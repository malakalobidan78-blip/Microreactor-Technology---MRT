01 / WHAT IS MRT?

A REACTOR.
REDUCED TO ITS PATHWAYS.

Microreactors typically contain channels with characteristic dimensions below approximately 1 mm, although the exact definition can vary with reactor design and application. Fluids move through engineered pathways where mixing, heat transfer and chemical reactions can be controlled at small scales.

04 / MICROMIXING

SHORTER PATHS.
FASTER MIXING.

At microscale dimensions, molecules may need to travel only short distances before mixing. Diffusion can therefore play an important role in micromixing, while the actual mixing behavior also depends on flow conditions and reactor geometry.

07 / TRANSPORT

CONTROL
THE HEAT.

Microscale channels can provide a high surface-area-to-volume ratio, which can support efficient heat exchange and thermal control. Actual performance depends on geometry, materials and operating conditions.

HEAT TRANSFER

High surface-area-to-volume ratios can support rapid thermal exchange in appropriately designed microscale systems.

MASS TRANSFER

Shorter diffusion paths can reduce the distance over which species must travel, supporting mass transfer and micromixing.

08 / GREEN CHEMISTRY

WHY GO
MICRO?

Microreactor technology is not automatically “green”. However, when the complete process is appropriately designed, microscale processing can support more resource-efficient chemistry.

01

SMALL MATERIAL QUANTITIES

Small reaction volumes can reduce the amount of material present during experimentation and process development.

02

RAPID MIXING

Microscale pathways can enable short diffusion distances and controlled mixing.

03

HEAT TRANSFER

Small dimensions can support efficient thermal exchange in appropriately designed systems.

04

PROCESS CONTROL

Flow, temperature and residence time can be controlled within a defined reaction environment.

10 / MATERIALS

MATERIALS.
PRECISION.

Microreactors can be fabricated from different materials. Material selection depends on chemical compatibility, temperature, pressure, mechanical requirements and whether optical access is needed.

BOROSILICATE GLASS

Transparent and useful for optical observation, with good chemical and thermal properties for selected systems.

QUARTZ

A transparent material used where optical access and material compatibility are important.

SILICON

Widely used in microfabrication and microfluidic technologies, especially for precisely structured devices.

POLYMERS

Polymeric materials can offer fabrication flexibility and are used in various microscale fluidic systems.

METALS / CERAMICS

Metals and ceramics can be selected when strength, temperature resistance or specific chemical compatibility is required.

12 / DON'T CONFUSE THEM

MICROFLUIDICS
AND MICROREACTORS.

Microfluidics is a broader field concerned with manipulating small fluid volumes and flows. Microreactor technology focuses specifically on microscale reactor systems for controlled chemical reactions. A microreactor can therefore be implemented as a microfluidic reactor.

MICROFLUIDICS

BROADER FIELD

Focuses on manipulating and controlling small volumes of fluids at microscale dimensions.

  • FLUID MANIPULATION
  • FLOW CONTROL
  • MICROSCALE SYSTEMS

13 / NUMBERING-UP

DON'T JUST
SCALE UP.

Instead of simply enlarging one microchannel, production capacity can be increased by operating multiple channels or reactor modules in parallel. This approach is commonly described as numbering-up or scale-out.

14 / GLOSSARY

KNOW THE
LANGUAGE.

01

Microreactor

A microscale reactor containing precisely defined channels or structures used to control fluid flow and chemical reactions.

02

Microchannel

A small engineered pathway through which fluids move inside the device.

03

Micromixing

Mixing occurring within microscale pathways, where diffusion and fluid motion can strongly influence the mixing process.

04

Diffusion

Molecular transport caused by random molecular motion, generally from regions of higher concentration toward regions of lower concentration.

05

Residence Time

The time material remains within a reactor. For a simple system, nominal residence time is related to reactor volume and volumetric flow rate by τ = V/Q.

06

Flow Rate

The volume of fluid passing through a specified point per unit time.

07

Heat Transfer

Transfer of thermal energy between regions of different temperature.

08

Mass Transfer

Transport of chemical species from one location to another within or between phases.

09

Numbering-Up

Increasing production capacity by operating multiple microreactor channels or modules in parallel.
