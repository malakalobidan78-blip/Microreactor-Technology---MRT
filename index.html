01 / WHAT IS MRT?

A REACTOR.
REDUCED TO ITS PATHWAYS.

A microreactor is a device containing precisely defined microscale features, commonly much smaller than 1 mm. Fluids travel through engineered channels where mixing and chemical reactions can be controlled at very small scales.

08 / GREEN CHEMISTRY

WHY GO
MICRO?

Microreactor technology is not automatically “green”. However, when the complete process is appropriately designed, microscale processing can support more resource-efficient chemistry.

01

SMALL MATERIAL QUANTITIES

Small reaction volumes can reduce the amount of material present during experimentation and process development.

02

RAPID MIXING

Microscale pathways can enable short diffusion distances and controlled mixing.

03

HEAT TRANSFER

Small dimensions can support efficient thermal exchange in appropriately designed systems.

04

PROCESS CONTROL

Flow, temperature and residence time can be controlled within a defined reaction environment.

14 / GLOSSARY

KNOW THE
LANGUAGE.

01 Microreactor +
A microscale reactor containing precisely defined channels or structures used to control fluid flow and chemical reactions.
02 Microchannel +
A small engineered pathway through which fluids move inside the device.
03 Micromixing +
Mixing occurring within microscale pathways, where diffusion and fluid motion can strongly influence the mixing process.
04 Diffusion +
Molecular transport caused by random molecular motion, generally from regions of higher concentration toward regions of lower concentration.
05 Residence Time +
The time material remains within a reactor. For a simple system, it is related to reactor volume and volumetric flow rate.
06 Flow Rate +
The volume of fluid passing through a specified point per unit time.
07 Heat Transfer +
Transfer of thermal energy between regions of different temperature.
08 Mass Transfer +
Transport of chemical species from one location to another within or between phases.
09 Numbering-Up +
Increasing production capacity by operating multiple microreactor channels or modules in parallel.
