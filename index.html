CHIP-TYPE MICROREACTOR

Microreactor
Technology

A chip-type microreactor is a miniaturized chemical-processing device with engineered small-scale flow pathways for mixing, transport, and chemical reaction.

Miniaturization reduces characteristic transport distances and can enable controlled flow, mixing, heat transfer, and reaction conditions.

01 — CONCEPT

What exactly is a microreactor?

The focus here is the chip-type microreactor: a device containing engineered small-scale flow pathways through which fluids can be transported, mixed, and reacted.

01

Miniaturized pathways

Microreactors often contain characteristic dimensions in the submillimeter range, although definitions vary across the literature and applications.

02

Small material inventory

The internal volume of the reaction pathways can be very small, so only a relatively small quantity of material is present inside the reactor at a given time.

03

Transport control

Small characteristic dimensions can strongly influence mixing, heat transfer, mass transfer, and nominal space-time behavior.

03 — PROCESS

What happens inside?

Mixing, mass transfer, heat transfer, and chemical reaction can occur within the same microscale flow pathway and may overlap in time and space.

REACTANTS

Reactant streams enter the microreactor.

MIXING

The streams interact within the microchannel geometry.

TRANSPORT

Mass and heat transport occur over small characteristic distances.

REACTION

Chemical transformation may occur while the reactants flow through the reactor.

PRODUCT

The processed stream exits through the outlet.

04 — MICROMIXING

Why can mixing be fast?

At small scales, diffusion can become important because the distance over which molecules must diffuse is reduced. However, diffusion is not the only possible mixing mechanism.

Diffusion scaling

A characteristic diffusion time scales approximately with the square of the characteristic diffusion distance.

tdiff ∝ L² / D
Scaling relationship; the exact prefactor depends on geometry and the physical model.

Mixing mechanisms

Microfluidic and microreactor mixing can involve passive or active mechanisms. Passive designs use channel geometry and flow behavior, while active designs introduce an external perturbation or energy input.

Small diffusion distance

Reducing the characteristic distance can reduce the characteristic diffusion time.

Channel geometry

Splitting, recombination, interfaces, and other geometrical features can enhance mixing.

Flow conditions

Mixing behavior depends on geometry, flow rate, fluid properties, and the relevant transport regime.

05 — TRANSPORT PHENOMENA

The physics behind the chip

Microreactor performance is strongly connected to transport phenomena.

Mass transfer

Small dimensions and engineered interfaces can influence how rapidly chemical species move between regions of the flow.

Heat transfer

High surface-area-to-volume ratios can facilitate heat transfer, depending on reactor design and operating conditions.

Nominal space time

For a simple steady-flow system, the nominal space time is estimated from reactor volume divided by volumetric flow rate. It is a nominal holding-time measure and is not necessarily the actual residence time of every fluid element.

τ = V / Q

08 — MATERIALS

What are microreactors made from?

Material selection depends on chemical compatibility, temperature, pressure, fabrication method, optical requirements, and intended application.

OPTICALLY TRANSPARENT

Borosilicate glass

Commonly used in microfluidic and microreactor fabrication because of its optical transparency and useful chemical and thermal properties.

OPTICALLY TRANSPARENT

Quartz

Useful where optical transparency and chemical or thermal properties are important.

OTHER MATERIALS

Polymers, silicon & metals

Other materials are also used depending on fabrication requirements, mechanical properties, chemical compatibility, and operating conditions.

09 — GREEN CHEMISTRY

Why is MRT relevant to green chemistry?

Microreactor technology can contribute to greener processing in appropriate applications, but a microreactor is not automatically a green process.

Smaller material inventory

Small internal volumes can reduce the quantity of hazardous material present inside the reactor at a given moment.

Process intensification

Enhanced transport and controlled process conditions can improve process performance in suitable reactions.

Potential safety benefits

Small inventories and efficient heat transfer can be advantageous for some hazardous or highly exothermic reactions.

Important scientific point

The environmental performance of a process must be evaluated using the actual chemistry, reagents, solvents, energy use, waste generation, separation requirements, and overall process design.

12 — IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

Microfluidics ≠ Microreactor

MICROFLUIDICS

Broader field

Microfluidics deals with the manipulation and transport of fluids in microscale systems and includes many applications that do not involve chemical reaction.

MICROREACTOR

Chemical-processing device

A microreactor is a small-scale chemical-processing device that uses microstructured flow pathways, such as microchannels, to carry out chemical reactions and related transport processes. The chip-type microreactor shown here is one important design form within the broader field of microreactor technology.

14 — GLOSSARY

Key terms

Microchannel — A small flow pathway used to transport fluids in a microscale device.
Micromixer — A microscale device or channel structure designed to enhance mixing.
Diffusion — Molecular transport caused by random molecular motion and concentration gradients.
Nominal Space Time — The ratio of reactor volume to volumetric flow rate, τ = V/Q.
Reynolds Number — A dimensionless quantity comparing inertial and viscous effects in fluid flow. Its value depends on fluid properties, velocity, characteristic length, and geometry.
Numbering-up — Increasing throughput by operating multiple microreactor channels or modules in parallel.
